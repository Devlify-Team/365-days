# Day 9 — Explore awesome lists

**Workbook mapping:** Day 9 · **Open Source** · Month 1: **Foundations & GitHub Basics** · primary: [GitHub awesome-lists](https://github.com/sindresorhus/awesome) · alternative: [GitLab awesome-list topics](https://gitlab.com/explore/projects/topics/awesome-list) · suggested repo: `explore-awesome-lists`.

## Goal

Learn how curated “awesome” lists help you discover tools, libraries, communities, and further reading. Treat them as starting points—not rankings, audits, or guarantees. The companion directory, [awesome-lists.md](awesome-lists.md), gives four examples to triage.

## A practical process

1. Pick a topic you genuinely want to learn: language, framework, data tool, self-hosted service, or workflow.
2. Open a relevant list and scan its scope, categories, README, contribution guide, and license.
3. For a candidate link, visit the project’s own repository or site. Check its documentation, releases/activity, issue discussions, supported versions, dependencies, license, and security guidance.
4. Compare multiple candidates against your needs. Record why a link is useful and what remains uncertain.
5. Prefer maintained, clearly scoped, well-documented projects—but do **not** rank lists or projects by stars alone. Activity, stars, and labels can change and do not establish quality, safety, or endorsement.
6. Do not copy list content or license text into your notes or a new repository. Link to the source and follow its contribution rules. Do not assume that being listed means a project was security audited.

## Curation criteria

The official [awesome manifesto](https://github.com/sindresorhus/awesome/blob/main/awesome.md) recommends curation rather than collection: include personally recommended quality, explain why each entry is useful, keep a clear scope, provide contribution guidelines, use good grammar, and include a license. Apply those criteria critically; public repository content and status may change.

## Manual exercises

- Choose one topic and identify two lists; write each list’s scope and one limitation.
- Select three candidate projects. Verify each project link, category fit, documentation, maintenance signals, license, contribution guidance, and basic safety considerations.
- Make a short comparison that states what you checked and what you could not verify.
- Optional: draft a tiny, clearly scoped list with explanations and source links, without reproducing another list.

## Limitations

This is descriptive exploration, not a personal endorsement, ranking, security review, or freshness guarantee. GitHub and GitLab discovery pages use changing metadata; GitLab topic labels are user tagging, not endorsement. No repository was created and no contribution was submitted for this exercise.
